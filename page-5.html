The Question of Favorites
2 octobre 1771
I have been advised that it is unwise to appear to enjoy certain conversations more than others.

This is difficult. Unfair, even. But I dare not complain further.

Some people are easier to speak with. Some people laugh more freely. Some people do not make me feel as though I am being evaluated by invisible mathematics.

I did not realize that kindness could be interpreted as strategy.

I also did not realize that friendships could be monitored in this way.

I am told that having “favorites” may cause imbalance.

I will try to have no expression at all going forward, just in case.

— Marie
A Small Misunderstanding
25 juin 1771
I apologize that I haven't updated in a while. That's because life in the palace just happens to be so fabulous it's a given that every day would eventually start to feel the same.

And yet I find myself here now because today I was informed that I may have unintentionally offended someone. What, me? This lovely queen to be? How could someone of such grace possibly... offend?

I am still trying to determine when this occurred. If it even has.

Apparently, during a conversation I nodded at the wrong time, smiled too widely at the wrong person, and stood in a location that is “politically sensitive.”

I was not aware that standing could be politically divisive.

I have apologized for all three things, though I am still not entirely certain which apology corresponded to which offense.

I am beginning to suspect that it may be safest to simply remain indoors and avoid becoming a geographical issue... but I still think I did nothing wrong.

— Marie
I Went For a Stroll
10 julliet 1770
Today I attempted to take a simple walk in the gardens without causing any confusion.

This proved to be more difficult than expected.

I was informed that walking “alone” is not truly alone if one is within sight of three courtiers, two gardeners, and a distant observer with a notebook.

At one point I believe I was being followed by a man whose entire job appeared to be adjusting the direction of sunlight.

I am still learning the rules of when one is permitted to exist casually.

Several people greeted me during this walk, all with slightly different interpretations of how deeply one must bow to prevent diplomatic consequences.

I smiled at everyone equally so as not to accidentally start a war.

I am told this was “correct behavior,” though I am still unsure.

— Marie
Court Fashion Is Terrifying
3 juin 1770
I thought I understood fashion before arriving at Versailles.

I did not.

This morning I watched a woman spend nearly two hours constructing what appeared to be an entire architectural project atop her head. Ribbons, feathers, jewels, lace, and at least one object that may have once belonged to a bird were involved.

Everyone here somehow walks gracefully while carrying enough powder to survive a small snowstorm.

I am trying very hard to learn everything properly, but I confess I am frightened of accidentally committing some horrible etiquette crime. Yesterday I was informed I held a teacup incorrectly for nearly twenty minutes while three duchesses silently watched me suffer.

France is very glamorous... and also very terrifying. I think I'm starting to understand why everyone walks so slowly.

— Marie
Bonjour, France!
16 mai 1770
Hi France!!

I have arrived safely at Versailles, and everything here is… so grand I can hardly believe it. The gardens feel like something out of a dream, and everyone is so properly dressed I feel as though I must practice walking more carefully just to keep up.

Everything is very new to me, and I hope very much to become worthy of this beautiful country and its people. I have been told many rules already, which I am doing my best to remember (there are quite a few!).

I miss Austria, of course, but I am told France will soon feel like home. I look forward to learning, listening, and becoming part of this court properly.

If anyone has advice for a new arrival… I am listening! :]

— Marie
